Asthma and Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

Asthma is a chronic inflammatory airway disease with variable, largely reversible airflow obstruction. Typical symptoms are wheeze, breathlessness, chest tightness and cough that vary over time and are often worse at night.

COPD is characterised by persistent airflow limitation that is not fully reversible. The diagnosis is confirmed by spirometry showing a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio below 0.70. Cigarette smoking is the main cause of COPD, and smoking cessation is the single most effective intervention to slow its progression.

In asthma, inhaled corticosteroids such as budesonide are the cornerstone of long-term control. Short-acting beta-2 agonists such as salbutamol (albuterol) relieve acute symptoms but should not be used alone as long-term treatment.

In COPD, long-acting bronchodilators (LAMA and LABA) are the mainstay of maintenance therapy. Pulmonary rehabilitation improves exercise capacity and quality of life. Long-term oxygen therapy improves survival in patients with severe resting hypoxaemia.
